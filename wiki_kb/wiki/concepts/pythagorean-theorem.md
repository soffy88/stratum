# Pythagorean Theorem

The Pythagorean Theorem states that in a right triangle, the square of the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares of the two legs:

**a² + b² = c²**

where **c** is the hypotenuse, the side opposite the right angle.

## Core idea

This theorem is a fundamental tool for measuring distances, proving triangles are right triangles, and solving geometric problems involving lengths, diagonals, areas, and coordinates. Its converse is also important: if three side lengths satisfy a² + b² = c², then the triangle is a right triangle.

## Typical uses shown in the workbook

### 1. Solving side lengths
When two sides of a right triangle are known, the third can be found directly:

- if both legs are known, find the hypotenuse with c = √(a² + b²)
- if the hypotenuse and one leg are known, find the other leg with a = √(c² - b²)

### 2. Proving a triangle is right
The workbook includes converse-type problems where three side lengths are compared. If the square relation holds, the triangle is confirmed to be right-angled.

### 3. Geometry proofs
Several solutions use the theorem inside larger proofs, especially when:

- a perpendicular is introduced to split a figure into right triangles
- congruent triangles are established using side relationships
- diagonals of quadrilaterals are analyzed
- lengths in rectangles, rhombi, and squares are connected through right triangles

### 4. Coordinate and applied problems
The theorem is also used to:

- compute distances on a grid
- analyze shortest paths
- solve practical geometry questions involving movement, layout, or measurement
