# Hartshorne roadmap for algebraic geometry

This reference captures the practical sequence of ideas encoded in the source material:

1. **Start with varieties and affine/projective geometry.**
   - Understand zero sets, coordinate rings, regular functions, rational maps, and function fields.
   - Use the Nullstellensatz, dimension theory, and projective embeddings as the first geometric toolkit.

2. **Move to schemes and sheaves.**
   - Replace points by prime ideals and regular functions by structure sheaves.
   - Treat morphisms locally on affine opens and use stalks/local rings as the pointwise test.

3. **Use divisors and line bundles to organize codimension-one geometry.**
   - Translate zeros/poles into divisors.
   - Convert divisors into invertible sheaves when line-bundle language is cleaner.
   - Read linear systems as projective spaces of sections.

4. **Use cohomology to control global behavior.**
   - Vanishing tells you when sections exist and when maps are generated.
   - Duality and Riemann–Roch turn geometry into numerical formulas.
   - Formal functions, semicontinuity, and base change control families.

5. **For birational geometry, expect blow-ups and intersection theory.**
   - Rational maps become regular after suitable modifications.
   - Surfaces factor through monoidal transformations.
   - Intersection numbers and self-intersections distinguish exceptional curves and ampleness.

6. **For curves and surfaces, classification is the goal.**
   - Curves are controlled by genus, divisors, and Jacobians.
   - Surfaces need minimal models, ruled surfaces, cubic surfaces, and the Kodaira dimension.

7. **Keep the geometric-algebraic dictionary in both directions.**
   - Translate from geometry to rings when proving statements.
   - Translate back to geometry when interpreting the result.

This roadmap is deliberately Hartshorne-centric: it reflects the book’s habit of proving concrete geometric claims by passing through local algebra, cohomology, and line-bundle language.
