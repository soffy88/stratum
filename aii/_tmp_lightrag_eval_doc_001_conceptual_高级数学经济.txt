# Policy classification (stationary, deterministic, policy sets ΠNM/ΠM/ΠS)

Type: conceptual | Subject: 高级数学经济

Policy Classification (Stationary, Deterministic, Policy Sets)
Type: Conceptual
Plain-language gloss:
A policy is like a recipe that tells an agent exactly how to choose actions in every possible situation. In MDPs, policies are classified based on two key factors:
information they use to decide:
Markovian policies only look at the *current state* (like making a decision based solely on where you are right now).
Non-Markovian policies use the *entire history* of states and actions (like considering every step you’ve taken so far).
they make decisions:
Deterministic policies always pick the same action in a given state (no randomness).
Stochastic policies randomly choose actions based on probabilities (like rolling a dice to decide).
Rigorous definition:
A policy π is a sequence of decision rules. A decision rule maps either the current state *s* (Markovian) or the history *h_t = (s₀, a₀, ..., s_t)* (non-Markovian) to a distribution over actions (stochastic) or a single action (deterministic):
Non-Markovian stochastic: π_t : S × (S × A)^t → Δ(A)
Markovian stochastic: π_t : S → Δ(A)
Non-Markovian deterministic: π_t : S × (S × A)^t → A
Markovian deterministic: π_t : S → A
Policy sets:
ΠNM: All non-Markovian policies
ΠM: All Markovian policies
ΠS: Stationary policies (same decision rule at every timestep)
Π^D NM, Π^D M, Π^D S: Deterministic subsets of each class.
Plain-language gloss:
These classifications matter because they trade off *generality* (solving complex problems) vs. *tractability* (computational ease):
Non-Markovian policies are the most flexible (they use full history) but are harder to compute.
Markovian policies are simpler (only current state) and often sufficient for optimal solutions.
Stationary policies avoid time-dependent rules, simplifying analysis.
Deterministic policies are easier to implement (no randomness) but may miss robust solutions.
Rigorous rationale:
The chapter emphasizes:
1. ΠNM is the most general class, encompassing all possible policies.
2. ΠS ⊂ ΠM ⊂ ΠNM: Stationary policies are Markovian, which are a subset of non-Markovian.
3. Deterministic subsets (Π^D NM, Π^D M, Π^D S) exist within each class for specialized applications.
Plain-language gloss:
Policies are structured hierarchically:
1. Decision rules build policies: Each rule defines how to act at a specific time.
2. Stationarity means using the same rule forever, avoiding the need to update decisions over time.
3. Determinism removes randomness: For example, a deterministic Markovian policy is a function *π: S → A*.
Rigorous structure:
1. Policy hierarchy:
Any Markovian policy (ΠM) can be expressed as π = (π, π, ...) (stationary).
Stationary policies (ΠS) are a strict subset: ΠS ⊂ ΠM.
2. Deterministic policies:
For non-Markovian: π_t(h_t) ∈ A (direct action output).
For Markovian: π(s) ∈ A (direct action output).
