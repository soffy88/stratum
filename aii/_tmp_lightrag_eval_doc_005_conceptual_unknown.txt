Optimal Controlled Trajectories Must Belong to Case B
Type: Conceptual
Optimal controlled trajectories must belong to Case B.
Gloss:
Case B: A class of dynamical systems where the system’s long-term behavior is neither entirely stable nor unstable (i.e., not *hyperbolic*), allowing flexibility for optimization.
Hyperbolic system: A system where solutions near an equilibrium split into stable (converging) and unstable (diverging) directions, and these properties are structurally stable under small perturbations.
Rigorous Statement
From, Proposition 4.2 implies that if the uncontrolled system is in *Case C* (defined by the existence of a set of positive Lebesgue measure where the control input \( u > 0 \)), and a control \( u \) drives the system to *Case A* (a hyperbolic regime), then \( u \) cannot be optimal. By contrapositive, optimality requires the controlled system to avoid *Case A*, i.e., to belong to *Case B*.
Because systems in Case A are hyperbolic, and hyperbolicity guarantees the existence of better controls via perturbations.
Gloss:
Lebesgue measure: A mathematical way to assign a "size" (e.g., length, area) to sets, generalizing volume in infinite-dimensional spaces.
Robustness under perturbations: If a system’s behavior remains qualitatively unchanged under small parameter changes, it is robust. Hyperbolic systems are robust.
Rigorous Argument
Let the uncontrolled system be in *Case C*. If a control \( u \) induces *Case A* (hyperbolic), then by Theorem 3.5, the system is hyperbolic. By Proposition 3.3, hyperbolic systems are robust: small perturbations \( \epsilon \in (0, \delta) \) preserve hyperbolicity. This implies \( u \) cannot be optimal because perturbing \( u \) would still yield a hyperbolic system, contradicting optimality (Proposition 4.2 proof,). Thus, optimal controls must avoid *Case A* (hyperbolic), leaving *Case B* as the only viable option.
By reformulating the optimal control problem (OCP) into an unconstrained optimization problem and ensuring the control avoids Case A.
Gloss:
Unconstrained optimization: Optimizing without restrictions (e.g., no inequality constraints like \( u \geq 0 \)).
Robustness: Solutions that remain valid even if inputs are slightly perturbed.
Rigorous Procedure
1. Reformulate (OCP) into an unconstrained problem using dynamical properties of optimal controls.
2. Approximate the optimal control as \( \tilde{u}(t) = \alpha(t) - g(t, \tilde{K}(g)) \), where \( \tilde{K}(g) \) captures system dynamics and \( g(t, \cdot) \) penalizes deviations from optimality.
3. Compare this approximation with IPOPT-generated solutions (constrained optimization) to verify that trajectories remain in *Case B* (non-hyperbolic) and avoid *Case A* (hyperbolic).
