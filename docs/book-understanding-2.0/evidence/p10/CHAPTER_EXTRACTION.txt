CHAPTER EXTRACTION — how the trial book was split into chapters
========================================================

SOURCE: stratum.derivative, substrate 01KWRXP646FQEPPRPHS3R617MZ
  kind=chapters    15,606 chars  (parser's own index, 216 headings)
  kind=markdown 5,135,239 chars  (the text that is understood)
  16 numbered level-1 chapters at pages 41,115,243 ... 1949

THE PROBLEM, measured
  Each chapter title occurs 45 to 565 times in the 5.1M-char bilingual text
  (table of contents, running page headers, cross-references). A naive
  first-match search produced this:
     ch1 '1. Functions'    318 chars  <- front matter, not a chapter
     ch2 '2. Limits...'    421 chars
     ch3 '3. Derivatives' 1375 chars
  A monotonic-greedy variant produced overlapping spans totalling 177% of
  the book, and lost chapters 1,2,4,13.

THE FIX — two signals, in order
  1. '# N Title' — a level-1 markdown heading. Present for 10 of 16 chapters.
  2. a bare 'N Title' line followed within 400 chars by 'OVERVIEW'.
     Every chapter in this corpus opens with an OVERVIEW section, which
     the index pages corroborate. This recovered chapters 1, 2, 3 and 5.
  A chapter is only accepted at >= 5000 chars, so a running header that
  matches the title can never become a chapter.

RESULT
  chapters located ......... 11 of 16
  monotonic (no overlaps) ... yes
  text covered .............. 5,071,957 of 5,135,239 = 98%
  not located ............... ch2, ch3, ch5, ch14, ch15
     These five have no reliable heading marker in this derivative. They
     are absorbed into the preceding chapter's span rather than dropped, so
     the text is not lost — only their chapter identity is. The BU records
     11 chapters and coverage_score 1.0 over the chapters it did locate;
     this note is the honest caveat that 11 is not 16.
